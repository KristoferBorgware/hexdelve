An enemy on a rig of its own. It sleeps on its hexagon with its wings wrapped round it; come within three tiles and it wakes, hunts you across the grid, and lunges off it to bite. Click to walk, click again to run — and it is slower than a run. There is a helmet, a sword and a shield lying in the yard: click one and he fetches it and puts it on. Armed, click the bat to go at it.

Its skeleton is not the humanoid one. Twenty bones, four to a wing, and the three folds outboard of the shoulder are what let two and a half metres of wing wrap up small enough to sit on one tile. Turn Skeletons on and it draws itself from that list — the same viewer that draws the man.

The gear is three props and no bones apiece — one group each, modelled around the origin of the bone it belongs to, so putting it on is a re-parent and every clip carries it afterwards. The cut is its own clip: inside-out, drawn across the body and swept back out to his right, so the edge leads and the point is never aimed at anything. Its reach and its bearing are measured from where the blade actually is at contact — the arc is out to the side, so a hit test that assumed "in front" would miss everything it hits.

It is not flying free: it paths over the same hexagons you walk, one A* at a time, and only leaves the grid for the same reason you do — to get within reach of something. Its wings clear two terraces in a step where you can only manage one.
