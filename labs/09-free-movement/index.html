W A S D to move, the mouse to face — and those are two different things. Forward is wherever you are pointing, so holding W and swinging the mouse curves him round, and holding A while you keep the mouse on the anvil walks him round it sideways, still watching it. Shift runs. Off the grid entirely: no path, no tiles to click, just a heading and a throttle. There is a helmet, a sword and a shield in the yard — walk over one and he picks it up — and the bat from lab 08 is asleep out past the anvil. Come within three tiles and it wakes and hunts you over the hexagons you are no longer standing on. Armed, click (or space) to cut: nothing aims it for you here, so back away with S, come round it with A and D, and put it in the arc yourself. On a phone, drag anywhere: your thumb is the stick.

Every character in labs 02–08 walked where it was looking, so one forward cycle was the whole of locomotion. Take that apart and a blend tree of forward clips has nothing to say: a man backing away from something he is watching is not playing a walk. The usual fix is three more clips — back, left, right — and a blend space over them.

Not here, because the walk was never a clip. It is a function of one phase angle (walk.js), so the direction of travel is simply another argument to it: the stride turns, and one cycle covers the whole circle of headings. What it cannot do is turn evenly — a leg swinging forward has the whole world in front of it, and a leg swinging sideways has the other leg. So the stride is an ellipse, 0.36 m down the line of the body and 0.15 m across it, and a bearing gets the radius in its own direction.

Which is why the numbers in the readout are not round. The speed is measured off the pose, every frame, by asking where the planted foot is at the two contact keys: whatever ground it covers, he covers. A side-step comes out at about half a walk — not a handicap anyone typed in, just the room the other leg leaves — and the readout shows what is left over as foot slip, which is nothing at all down the cardinals and a few centimetres a second on a running diagonal.

The bat is lab 08's, brought over whole — the same twenty bones, the same A* over the same hexagons, the same bite measured out of its lunge. It hunts a man who is no longer standing on a tile, and the only line that ever noticed is the one asking which cell he is in. What changed is your half of the fight: there the pathfinder walked you into range and held you facing it, and here nothing aims for you. The blade's reach and the bearings it sweeps between are measured off the clip, so a cut thrown at where it was misses.

The pelvis opens towards where he is going and the chest turns back, so his shoulders keep facing the mouse while his legs take their own heading. That is lab 08's upper-body mask argued the other way round. The cut is masked the same way while he moves: whole-body when he is standing, arms only when he is not, because his legs are busy.
