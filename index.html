Hexdelve

Hexagon experiments on a flat plane, viewed isometrically. Every object in every scene is a hexagonal prism — the terrain, the buildings, the characters, the helmet, the smoke. Each lab is a standalone page with no build step.

Labs

01 Log cabin Every object in this scene is a hexagonal prism. 02 Blacksmith rig A humanoid skeleton drives a character built from hexagonal prisms. 03 Keyframe player Clips, sampling, blending and events are plain data and plain math — Three.js only draws the result. 04 Blend tree One number drives the legs. Set a speed and the tree picks the clips, weights them, and lines their footfalls up. 05 Inverse kinematics The clips only know joint angles, so contact is wherever the angles happen to land. IK solves the other way: name the point, get the angles. 06 Click to move Click a hexagon to walk there. Click again while he is going and he runs. Click the anvil and he walks to the nearest tile beside it, steps off the grid to get within reach, strikes once, and steps back onto his hexagon. 07 Player character You are the wanderer — a second character on the same 17-bone rig, carrying nothing. Click a hexagon to walk there, click again to run. Click the smith or his anvil and you walk over to watch him work. Click the helmet lying in the yard and he fetches it and puts it on; click him again to take it off. 08 The bat An enemy on a rig of its own. It sleeps on its hexagon with its wings wrapped round it; come within three tiles and it wakes, hunts you across the grid, and lunges off it to bite. Click to walk, click again to run — and it is slower than a run. There is a helmet, a sword and a shield lying in the yard: click one and he fetches it and puts it on. Armed, click the bat — or the hexagon it is standing on, tinted red — and he runs to a tile beside it, facing it the whole way, and cuts. Neither of them can walk into a hexagon the other is standing in. 09 Free movement W A S D to move, the mouse to face — and those are two different things. Forward is wherever you are pointing, so holding W and swinging the mouse curves him round, and holding A while you keep the mouse on the anvil walks him round it sideways, still watching it. Shift runs. Off the grid entirely: no path, no tiles to click, just a heading and a throttle. There is a helmet, a sword and a shield in the yard — walk over one and he picks it up — and the bat from lab 08 is asleep out past the anvil. Come within three tiles and it wakes and hunts you over the hexagons you are no longer standing on. Armed, click (or space) to cut: nothing aims it for you here, so back away with S, come round it with A and D, and put it in the arc yourself. On a phone, drag anywhere: your thumb is the stick.
Open any lab and drag to orbit, wheel to zoom, right-drag to pan. Most read their initial state from the query string, e.g. labs/04-blend-tree/index.html?speed=1.4&skel=1.
